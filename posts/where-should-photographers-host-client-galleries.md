Where you host client photos is one of the most important choices in your business, because it is the last thing a client sees. It is also a separate decision from how you run everything else.

## What a gallery host needs to do

Whatever you choose, check these basics before you commit:

- **Handles full-resolution files.** A wedding can be hundreds of images, and you do not want to compress them to fit.
- **Easy for clients to use.** They should be able to view, favorite and download from a phone without creating an account or asking you for help.
- **Backed up.** The host should not be your only copy. Keep your own originals somewhere safe.
- **Looks like you.** A clean, branded gallery reflects well on your work.
- **Fair storage terms.** Look at what happens to your galleries if you stop paying or switch plans.

## Dedicated gallery services

Services built for photographers, such as Pixieset and ShootProof, are designed around delivery. They give clients a polished viewing experience, favoriting, downloads and often print sales. For weddings and larger sessions, this is usually the better fit.

## Cloud drives

Google Drive, Dropbox and similar services are fine for quick handoffs and small sessions. They are flexible and familiar, but they are not built for browsing a large photo set, and clients can find them clunky on a phone.

## Why your business app should not host photos

Photo hosting is heavy, expensive and a specialty of its own. The tools that do it well put all their effort into it.
