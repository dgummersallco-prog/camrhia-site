A photography business needs more than a camera. Between the first inquiry and the final photos, you are handling communication, contracts, payments, scheduling, and delivery. The tools below cover those jobs. Some apps do one job well, and some bundle several.

## The short answer

Most photography businesses need tools in eight categories:

1. Editing software
2. A portfolio website
3. Client and inquiry management
4. Contracts
5. Payments and invoicing
6. A calendar and scheduling
7. Gallery delivery
8. Accounting and backups

## 1. Editing software

Adobe Lightroom and Capture One are the most common choices for editing and organizing photos, and many photographers add Photoshop for retouching. Pick the one that fits your workflow and learn it well.

## 2. A portfolio website

Clients need somewhere to see your work and contact you. Website builders like Squarespace and Format are common, and gallery platforms like Pixieset include website options. Camrhia includes a live portfolio website with your subscription. The main things to look for are how easy it is to update, whether clients can inquire from the page, and whether it looks like your work.

## 3. Client and inquiry management

This is where inquiries, leads, and ongoing client communication live. HoneyBook and Dubsado are widely used for this and also handle contracts and invoices. If you handle a small number of clients, you can start with a spreadsheet, but it gets harder as you grow.

## 4. Contracts

You should have a contract for every job. Many client-management tools include contract templates and e-signatures. For what a contract should cover, see our guide on [how to write a wedding photography contract](/blog/how-to-write-a-wedding-photography-contract).

## 5. Payments and invoicing

You need a clear way to collect a deposit and the remaining balance, and to see who has paid. Look for a payment schedule that tracks what is due and when, and reminders so you are not chasing people by hand. See [how a wedding photography payment schedule works](/blog/how-does-a-wedding-photography-payment-schedule-work).

## 6. A calendar and scheduling

A calendar that holds your shoots, deadlines, and deliveries in one place keeps double bookings from happening. Some tools sync with Google Calendar or Apple Calendar, and some include their own.

## 7. Gallery delivery

Pixieset, ShootProof, and Pic-Time are popular gallery platforms, and plenty of photographers use Google Drive or Dropbox. Whatever you use, the goal is a clean handoff where the client knows exactly where to find their photos. Camrhia does not host galleries. It delivers your gallery link inside the client's app, next to the rest of their session.

## 8. Accounting and backups

Keep your income and expenses organized from the first paid job. QuickBooks or a simple spreadsheet is enough to start. Back up your files in at least two places, one of them off-site or in the cloud.

## One app or several tools?

Using several specialized tools means each one can be the best at its job, but you pay for each, learn each, and move information between them yourself. An all-in-one app is simpler and often cheaper overall, but may go less deep in any one area. If you notice you spend more time on admin than on photography, it is worth consolidating. Our guide to [choosing wedding photography business software](/blog/how-to-choose-wedding-photography-business-software) goes deeper on the tradeoffs.

## A simple way to choose

List what you spend time on every week. If the same three jobs eat your evenings, such as chasing payments, sending contracts, and answering the same client questions, start with a tool that handles those. Add the rest only when you need it.
